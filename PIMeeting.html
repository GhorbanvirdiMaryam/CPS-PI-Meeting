Poster and lightning talk: Prevent Information Leakage in Real-Time Systems

My experience

In March 2025 I travelled to Nashville for the NSF Cyber-Physical Systems Principal Investigators' Meeting, where NSF-funded CPS projects come together to share what they are working on. I was there to present our NSF CAREER project, Indistinguishability Prevents Information Leakage in Real-Time Schedulers, led by my advisor Sibin Mohan.

Before the meeting I recorded a 90-second lightning talk to introduce the project. At the meeting I stood next to our poster, number 50 in the program, and talked with researchers from many different corners of cyber-physical systems. My message was simple: real-time systems are predictable, that predictability can leak what the system is doing, and carefully chosen noise can hide it while the system still meets its deadlines.

About the work

  • Real-time schedulers are predictable, and an attacker can use that to learn what the system is doing.
  • Schedule indistinguishability: make it hard to tell one job's arrival from another.
  • Method: add Laplace noise to job inter-arrival times, inspired by differential privacy.
  • Current work: comparing DP variants and noise types, and testing a network-based timing side-channel attack.
